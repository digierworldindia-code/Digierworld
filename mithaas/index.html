Our Story

A Little Sweetness.
A Lot of Memories.

Nobody remembers the box. They remember who brought it, and what the house smelled like when it was opened.

That is really the whole business. A card arrives, a result comes through, someone gets married, someone comes home after a long time away — and before anyone says anything sensible, somebody is sent out for mithai. We have been on the other end of that errand for a while now, and we take it seriously.

So the kadhai goes on early. Chhena is set, khoya is stirred down, the oven is warm by the time the shutters go up. Through the day the counter is refilled rather than stocked once, because a laddoo made at four in the afternoon is a different thing from one made at six in the morning. In the kitchen behind it, dal sits on a low flame, dosa batter finishes fermenting, and lunch begins.

We would rather make less of something and make it properly.

The Restaurant

More Than Mithai.
A Table Full of Flavour.

Past the sweet counter there is a proper kitchen, and it is busier than most people expect. Breakfast is dosa and idli. Lunch is thali. By evening it is chaat at the front, noodles in the wok, and somebody's birthday cake coming out of the fridge.

Why Mithaas

What We Pay Attention To

01

Freshness

Sweets are made in small batches through the day, not once in the morning. What you take home was almost certainly made a few hours ago.

02

Craftsmanship

Dal makhani sits on low heat overnight. Dosa batter is ground and left to ferment. Some things can't be hurried, so we don't.

03

Variety

A box of mithai, a birthday cake, and dinner for four — without driving to three different places.

04

Hospitality

The counter staff know most faces and remember most orders. That's not a policy, it's just how it happens.
